Thirteen components carry the product's core loop — ask, read, quiz, unlock, celebrate, follow up. Three of them shape the first question; ten of them run the journey that follows.

QuestionInput: the primary action, defended

QuestionInput owns the product's single most important interaction, and most of its 225 lines are small acts of care around a textarea. The field auto-grows to 160 px via a useLayoutEffect that resets and re-measures scrollHeight on every keystroke. A draft-recovery pair of effects persists the typed question to sessionStorage under reallearn_draft_question and restores it on mount if the field is empty, so an accidental refresh loses nothing; on desktop widths (≥768 px) the field auto-focuses. Submission is Ctrl/Cmd+Enter (plain Enter inserts a newline), with a <kbd> hint that renders a neutral "Ctrl" until mount because navigator.platform differs between server and a Mac client — a hydration-mismatch fix recorded in a comment. Input is clamped to MAX_QUESTION_LENGTH = 1000 everywhere, including voice transcripts, which append programmatically and therefore bypass the textarea's own maxLength.

The mode toggle is a two-option role="radiogroup" styled as the .mode-glider, with an animated pill whose translateX is computed from the active index — one of the few legitimate inline styles, since it is state-driven. Fast promises "Straight answer, zero fluff"; Explain promises the "Full 3-part deep dive — quizzes included". Signed-out visitors do not get a dead button: the submit slot renders Clerk's <SignInButton mode="modal"> labeled "Sign in to Learn →", while signed-in users see the mode-aware label ("Get a Quick Answer →" or "Start Guided Lesson →").

HomeStats and ExampleQuestions: removing the blank-input problem

HomeStats exists, per its own comment, to remove "the blank-input friction that keeps people from re-engaging". Its centerpiece is a daily spark: a twenty-entry DAILY_TOPICS array ("How does my For You page decide what I see?", "Why can't we just print more money?") indexed by day-of-year, computed in UTC from calendar fields to dodge a daylight-saving off-by-one that the code documents explicitly. If an unfinished journey exists in savedJourneysStore — judged from index counts, since lesson bodies live in IndexedDB — a resume card loads the archived lesson with getArchivedLesson() and pushes to /learn without any network call, falling back to regeneration only when the archive copy is gone. New visitors with zero activity instead see the .how-strip: a three-step ordered list (ask, learn in three parts, pass each quiz). ExampleQuestions is the smallest component in the catalog: a chip that cycles ten curiosity-bait questions on a 3.5-second interval and pipes the current one into the input on click.

PartCard: where the lesson is read

PartCard renders one part of a journey and is memoized, since three of them re-render under a busy page. Its content pipeline is ReactMarkdown with remarkGfm, plus a components override that hardens every AI-generated link: forced target="_blank" with rel="noopener noreferrer", on top of react-markdown's default URL scheme stripping — a stored-XSS posture the file explains in a security comment. Above the prose sits a part badge, a subject chip tinted from a subjectColors map (--subject-physics through --subject-general, mixed to 12% backgrounds with color-mix), a one-tap "📋 Copy" button for the section text, and a ListenButton that reads the part aloud in the lesson's language. A TL;DR banner gives each part a one-line promise keyed to its number: fundamentals, mechanism, or real-world grounding.

The card's gating behavior is its real job. An active, uncompleted part runs useReadingTimer and shows a thin progress bar; until the timer completes, the quiz entry is a deliberately quiet "I already know this → Skip to quiz" text button, after which the full .part-cta appears — labeled "I've Read This → Continue" when the part has no quiz, so the UI never promises a quiz that will not open. Locked parts wrap their content in .part-locked-content, a 12 px blur plus fade that globals.css swaps for a cheap opacity drop on low-performance devices, under a glass overlay with a padlock icon and the instruction to finish the previous part. Completed parts collapse to a single .part-done-bar row showing the title and score.

QuizSheet and QuizQuestion: the gate itself

QuizSheet is a modal bottom sheet implementing the product's core rule — 100% to advance — without ever hardcoding quiz length. The total is derived from the actual question array because the backend can legitimately deliver a salvaged one-question quiz; a comment records that a hardcoded total of two once made such quizzes unpassable, stranding the learner. The sheet traps Tab focus manually, restores focus on close, and allows Escape only before an answer is selected. Its scoring subtlety matters for the whole engagement system: since passing requires a perfect run, the score at the moment of passing is always perfect — so the sheet captures firstAttemptScore on the first completed attempt and reports that through onPass, making "perfect part" statistics mean "aced on the first try". On a failed run, every question is passed through reshuffleQuestion(), a status line announces "Answers reshuffled — the correct one has moved. Find it again.", and a dotted-underline link offers to close the sheet and scroll back to the section for a re-read. The failure button is labeled "Run It Back".

QuizQuestion renders one question as an accessible radiogroup. Option badges are derived with String.fromCharCode(65 + index) rather than a fixed A–D table, because model-generated quizzes can exceed four options — the old table produced an "Option undefined" accessible name. Keyboard handling covers arrows, Enter/Space, digits 1–9, and letters A–Z within the option count. Wrong selections shake (animate-shake); the revealed correct answer pulses; and an explanation block renders below with role="alert".

Integrity of the quiz gate

Three components conspire to keep the gate honest. lib/quizShuffle.ts guarantees the correct answer lands in a different slot after every failure — a Fisher-Yates shuffle followed by a deterministic neighbor swap if the answer happened to stay put, so memorizing positions cannot substitute for understanding. QuizSheet reports first-attempt scores so retries improve mastery without inflating stats. And the learn page's credit keys (Chapter 07) make passes idempotent per lesson instance, so the gate cannot be farmed for XP. The shuffle even ships with its own verification script, npm run verify:quiz.

Completion, celebration, and the next question

CompletionScreen lazy-loads only after the final pass. Its score ring is an SVG circle whose strokeDashoffset animates against a circumference of 2π × 42; the denominator is the real quiz-question count, guarded to a minimum of one because an empty salvaged lesson once produced a NaN ring. Fifty confetti particles in seven brand colors fall for four seconds, then the screen settles into content: the headline ("Run Complete", or "Quick W" for one-part fast lessons), a first-try score sentence with tiered copy at the 80% and 50% marks, the QuickSummaryCards takeaway deck, and up to three follow-up suggestions generated from the topic and first takeaway. Clicking a suggestion scrolls to the follow-up box and dispatches a reallearn:fillFollowUp CustomEvent — which FollowUpBox listens for, filling its textarea with a length clamp. The screen also announces the result to screen readers through the shared #sr-live-region element.

ShareResult turns the score into a story-format image: it draws a 1080×1920 canvas by hand — a solid #121620 ground, a faint 54 px grid, wrapped question text via a custom wrapText helper, plus level, streak, and score pulled live from progressStore — and hands the blob to the Web Share API, with copy that reads "can you pass this? 💀". UnlockAnimation is the opposite extreme: 35 lines rendering two fixed overlay layers (animate-accent-flash and a glow burst) that the learn page shows for 850 ms after each pass. FeedbackPrompt, shown through FeedbackGate the day after the first completed lesson, collects a 1–10 star rating and two free-text answers, and posts them with deliberately no Authorization header — its comment states the contract: no Clerk ID, email, or IP is ever attached, and a local flag ensures the ask never repeats.

What the catalog says about the product

Read together, these thirteen components encode the product thesis in mechanism rather than copy. The homepage trio spends its complexity on lowering the cost of asking — drafts survive refreshes, examples rotate, yesterday's unfinished journey is one tap away. The learning seven spend theirs on making progress legible and earned: reading time is visualized before the quiz appears, quiz answers move when you fail, scores mean first attempts, and celebration is concentrated at real milestones instead of sprinkled over every click. The two shapes match the two halves of the loop: reduce friction going in, add meaning coming out.
